# PRODUCT.md — John Transfer

## Product purpose

John Transfer is a licensed private-driver service operating across Ireland: airport transfers in Dublin, Cork and Belfast, and full-day private tours out of Dublin. One driver, one car, fixed price agreed before the journey. Not a fleet, not an app, not a marketplace.

The website has one job: convert a traveller who is unsure about trusting a stranger's car into a booking request. Everything else is secondary.

## Register

**brand**

This is a marketing surface. The design *is* the product experience until the passenger gets in the car. There is no application behind it, no account, no dashboard. Persuasion and reassurance carry the page.

## Users

**Primary: the arriving traveller.** Lands at Dublin, Cork or Belfast airport, often early morning, often after a long flight. Phone in one hand, luggage in the other. Deciding in under sixty seconds whether to pre-book a private driver or risk the taxi rank. What they need, in order: is this real, what does it cost, who is driving, how do I lock it in.

**Secondary: the trip planner.** Booking two to four weeks out, comparing a private day tour against a coach tour. Wants duration, price and route certainty before they commit a full day of a short holiday.

**Tertiary: the returning customer.** Already knows John. Needs the phone number and the booking form fast, nothing else.

Almost all traffic is mobile, much of it in transit or on airport wifi. Design for a thumb, a small screen and a hurried reader.

## Tone

Direct, calm, specific. The voice of a driver who has done this a thousand times and does not need to oversell it.

- Concrete beats adjectival. "Dublin Airport to city centre, €84.90, 4 seater" outperforms "seamless luxury travel experience".
- Prices always visible, never "contact for a quote".
- Reassurance through detail, not adjectives: flight tracking, meet and greet, fixed price agreed upfront, 24×7.
- Never breathless. No "unforgettable", "breathtaking", "seamless", "elevate", "curated", "bespoke journey".
- No em dashes. No exclamation marks.
- English only. The audience is international travellers in Ireland.

## Strategic principles

1. **Price is the trust signal.** A named number shown before any form is what separates a real operator from a scam. Never hide it behind an interaction.
2. **One driver is the pitch, not a limitation.** Four seats maximum, one named person. Say it plainly; it is why the service is worth more than a taxi.
3. **The booking is a conversation, not a checkout.** The form starts a conversation with John. It should feel like sending a message, not completing a purchase.
4. **Mobile in transit is the design target.** If it does not work one-handed on a 375px screen on bad wifi, it does not work.
5. **Transfers and tours are different products.** Transfers compete on price and certainty and show vehicle capacity. Tours compete on the day itself and show duration. Never flatten them into one card grid.

## Anti-references

- **OTA aggregators** (Booking.com, GetYourGuide, Viator). Dense, badge-covered, urgency-baiting, ten competing CTAs. John is the opposite: one operator, one number, one action.
- **Generic Ireland tourism.** Emerald green on white, shamrocks, harp motifs, a Celtic knot border. The first-order category reflex and instantly forgettable.
- **Luxury chauffeur cliché.** Gold serif on black, "bespoke", a stock photo of a gloved hand on a door handle. Reads as a stock template, not a real person.
- **SaaS landing template.** Hero metric row, three identical icon cards, gradient text, glass panels, logo cloud. Would make a driver in Dublin look like a Series A startup.
- **Fake urgency.** "3 people viewing", countdown timers, "only 2 left". Corrodes the exact trust the page exists to build.
